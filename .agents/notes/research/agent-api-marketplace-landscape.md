# Win the demand side before Stripe does

As of October 2026 Zevium's whitespace is real, narrow, and closing. No scaled player combines four things: OpenAPI-native listing, one fiat prepaid wallet across vendors, an agent meta-MCP, and real publisher payouts. Every big MCP directory pays tool authors **$0**. RapidAPI takes **25%** and pays out through PayPal only, with up to a two-month lag. Apify pays about **80%**, but only for Actors hosted on Apify. Zevium's **95/5 is the highest documented split** in the market. The bad news: the plumbing is a commodity. Zuplo and Kong turn any OpenAPI route into a billable MCP tool. Cloudflare's Monetization Gateway charges per call over x402 on the same edge Zevium runs on. Stripe now owns OpenRouter (~$7.5B), Metronome, and MPP, which is every part of an "OpenRouter for APIs" except the non-LLM catalog. Agent payment rails carry hundreds of millions of transactions but tiny dollars, and much of it is wash traffic. So billing is not the fight. Demand is. Pick one wedge category where suppliers are substitutable (search, scrape, SERP). Get into the Claude Connectors Directory, the MCP Registry, and the rail directories. Accept x402/MPP as a top-up rail through Stripe. Sell hard spend caps plus gateway-measured quality as the trust layer. Seed supply with claimable spec listings and negotiated "house" listings. The biggest threats are Stripe, Cloudflare, and Apify, in that order.

## Every big tool catalog pays authors zero, and the payers are small

The landscape has four groups. None of them is Zevium. First, **SaaS-connector platforms** (Composio, Pipedream, Zapier MCP, Arcade) sell managed OAuth to apps the end user already pays for. Composio routes agents through meta-tools across "thousands of tools" ([Composio docs](https://docs.composio.dev/tool-router/overview)). Zapier bills two tasks per MCP call ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/pricing/rates)). Arcade charges $0.01 per tool call plus $0.10 per auth event ([Arcade](https://www.arcade.dev/pricing)). None of them onboards paid API vendors. A third party once filed a GitHub issue asking Composio for an 82% revenue share for tool providers. It went stale ([GitHub #2818](https://github.com/ComposioHQ/composio/issues/2818)). Second, **registries** (the official MCP Registry, Glama, mcp.so, Docker) are distribution channels, not rivals. Glama indexes **91,691 servers** and self-reports only "1M+ tool calls / month" ([glama.ai](https://glama.ai)). Third, **client app stores**: Anthropic's Connectors Directory and OpenAI's app directory. Fourth, **enterprise MCP gateways** (Cloudflare Portals, Kong, Docker, Permit), which sell governance, not commerce.

Consolidation is eating neutrality. Workday agreed to buy Pipedream in November 2025 ([Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2025-11-19-Workday-Signs-Definitive-Agreement-to-Acquire-Pipedream)). Arcade bought Smithery on 2026-08-05, and Forbes flagged "conflicts of interest, portability, and independent grading" ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/janakirammsv/2026/08/10/arcade-acquires-smithery-to-own-the-agent-tool-supply-chain/)). Composio's August 2026 repricing drew "gotcha" coverage for cutting included calls and adding separate meters ([Scalekit](https://www.scalekit.com/blog/composio-pricing-change)). An independent catalog with one simple meter is now a selling point by itself.

**Apify is the only scaled payer.** It sent more than **$1M** to community developers for March 2026 earnings ([Apify Discord](https://discord.apify.com/m/1397159285774880880)). It runs pay-per-event billing, keyless agent payments over x402 or Skyfire, and a meta-MCP server ([Apify docs](https://docs.apify.com/platform/actors/publishing/monetize)). The order matters: supply came after Apify already had scraping demand, not before. RapidAPI is the cautionary tale. It was valued near $1B in 2022, cut half its staff in 2023 after trying "to compete on too many fronts" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/25/rapidapi-valued-at-1-billion-last-year-cuts-staff-by-50/amp)), and was sold to Nokia, which is steering it toward telecom APIs ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/13/nokia-acquires-rapid-the-api-company-once-valued-at-1b/)). It raised its fee after the acquisition while its catalog stayed hard to search and its subscription gates blocked agents from choosing tools at runtime.

The strongest structural argument for OpenAPI-first supply: company-run remote MCP servers grew to only **1,412** by February 2026, under 1% of the **151K API subdomains** in the same company universe ([Bloomberry](https://bloomberry.com/blog/we-analyzed-1400-mcp-servers-heres-what-we-learned)). Most sellable APIs have no MCP server and won't write one. Zevium can list them from the spec they already have.

## Billing plumbing is now a free feature; demand is the only moat

Self-hosting monetization is cheap and getting cheaper. Zuplo's monetization entered public beta on 2026-03-26 ([Zuplo changelog](https://zuplo.com/changelog/2026/03/26/api-monetization-beta)). It ships prepaid credit packs with bonus tiers and auto-refill, edge 429 gating, and "any OpenAPI route into a billable MCP tool with no extra configuration", free up to **100K monetization events a month** ([Zuplo](https://zuplo.com/solutions/monetize-and-control)). Kong bought OpenMeter and generates MCP tools from REST paths ([Kong docs](https://developer.konghq.com/mcp/autogenerate-mcp-tools)). Billing engines are being absorbed into processors and gateways. Stripe closed its Metronome deal on 2026-01-14 ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-nl/newsroom/news/stripe-completes-metronome-acquisition)). WSO2 took Moesif ([WSO2](https://wso2.com/about/news/wso2-acquires-leading-api-analytics-and-monetization-startup-moesif/)). Adyen reportedly bought Orb for ~$335M, a figure that comes from rival Lago ([Lago](https://getlago.com/blog/orb-adyen-acquisition-lago-alternative)). Stripe Billing costs 0.7% of volume ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-cn/billing/pricing)). A small publisher on Zuplo Free plus Stripe pays roughly **3.5–4% all-in** against Zevium's 5% plus processing. That estimate comes from the research notes, not a published benchmark. **Zevium can't win on fee. The 5% has to buy demand.**

Every self-host tool sells plumbing and no buyers. Zuplo's portal covers one publisher only. Cloudflare's Monetization Gateway has no directory and no OpenAPI integration, so agents learn the price only when they hit a 402 ([Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/monetization-gateway/)). And **nobody declares per-operation pricing inside the spec**. Zuplo plans and Cloudflare rules are configured separately from the API description. `x-zevium-cost` is the one primitive nobody else has.

LLM routers show both the prize and the trap. OpenRouter passes inference through at list price and takes 5.5% on card top-ups ([OpenRouter FAQ](https://openrouter.ai/docs/faq)). Sacra estimates that reached **~$140M ARR by July 2026** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/openrouter-growing-29-mom-at-140m-year)). At 5%, that implies roughly $2.8B of GMV, which is only reachable with heavy, substitutable traffic. Per-token monetization also fell ~60% as cheap models took share, so an aggregator's revenue follows its cheapest supply. Per-call economics on data APIs are brutal. Agent search runs **$1–$16 per 1,000 calls** (Parallel $1–5, Exa $4–7, Tavily $8–16) ([Exa](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/pricing); [Parallel](https://docs.parallel.ai/getting-started/pricing); [Tavily](https://docs.tavily.com/guides/api-credits)). At those prices a 5% cut is a few hundredths of a cent per call. Only prepaid credits can collect that, which Zevium already does.

## Stripe, Cloudflare, and Apify are the biggest threats

**Stripe is the top threat.** It is buying OpenRouter for ~$7.5B, its largest deal ever ([The Stack](https://www.thestack.technology/stripe-acquires-openrouter-setting-up-a-battle-for-ai-routing-business/)). It owns Metronome. It co-authored MPP, which settles agent payments into an ordinary Stripe balance and works "for Connect platforms across all charge types" ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine)). It runs a seller Directory for agents and token billing with markup ([Stripe token billing](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/token-billing)). OpenRouter's web plugin already resells Exa, Parallel, and Perplexity on OpenRouter credits ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/plugins/web-search)). That is the seed of an API marketplace, and it now sits inside Stripe. The one missing piece is a catalog of non-LLM APIs with self-serve publisher payouts, which is exactly Zevium's product. Whether Stripe builds it is unknown. Zevium also runs on Stripe, so the same fact makes it a natural acquisition target.

**Cloudflare is the platform risk.** The Monetization Gateway prices routes and verbs, charges variable amounts up to a cap, and converts origin 401s into payable 402s with "no signup, no API key" ([Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/monetization-gateway/)). It is in closed beta for US sellers with USDC on Base ([Intelligent CIO](https://www.intelligentcio.com/me/2026/10/08/cloudflare-launches-monetization-gateway-beta-to-enable-usage-based-payments-for-ai-agents/)). Cloudflare already takes a 5% fee on AI Gateway credits ([Cloudflare docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/unified-billing/)), bought Replicate, and ships Code Mode and MCP Portals. Adding a directory would put it in competition with tenants like Zevium.

**Apify is the incumbent most likely to expand into Zevium's niche.** It already pays out, accepts x402 and MPP, runs a meta-MCP, and spent $60k in grand prizes to get 1,086 qualifying Actors in three months ([Apify challenge](https://apify.com/challenge)). Letting it proxy third-party APIs instead of only hosting them would be a small step for Apify (inference).

Crypto-native catalogs are loud but thin. Coinbase's Agentic.market launched with no approval process and 365+ services ([BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/04/22/coinbase-agentic-market-launch-x402-165m-transactions-base-agent-commerce/)). The underlying Bazaar showed **34,062 listings** on 2026-10-09. Only **420 had 10 or more payers**, and **94% lacked a "use when" line** ([DEV](https://dev.to/tanod/state-of-the-x402-bazaar-34062-listings-2158-hosts-and-94-missing-a-use-when-line-34co)). TRM Labs counted ~198.9M x402 settlements (~$52.7M) and attributed only **0.6–7.5% of the dollar amount to AI agents** ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/coinbase-processes-1t-in-stablecoin-payments-annually-x402-hits-160m-transactions)). x402 itself is now neutral. The x402 Foundation went operational under the Linux Foundation with Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, Google, AWS, and Cloudflare as premier members ([x402 Foundation](https://x402.org/linux-foundation-announces-operational-launch-of-x402-foundation-to-standardize-internet-native-payments-for-ai-agents-and-applications/)). Supporting it is cheap optionality, not a revenue engine.

The big data APIs won't list early. Exa ($2.2B valuation) and Parallel ($2B) have their own MCP servers and sell keyless over x402 or MPP ([Bloomberg Gov](https://news.bgov.com/antitrust/andreessen-backed-ai-search-startup-exa-valued-at-2-2-billion); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/29/parallel-web-systems-hits-2b-valuation-five-months-after-its-last-big-raise/); [Exa x402](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/x402-guide); [Stripe MPP](https://stripe.com/it/blog/machine-payments-protocol)). Clouds are buying search to bundle with inference. Nebius bought Tavily for up to $400M ([Nebius 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001513845/000110465926094844/nbis-20260812xex99d2.htm)). The last threat is store policy. OpenAI's Apps SDK limits approved monetization to physical goods ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/build/monetization)). Anthropic's policy on connectors gated by third-party credits was not found.

## The steal list goes past treg on supply, demand, and trust

### Supply: list APIs whose owners never touched Zevium

**Claimable spec listings** are the boldest move here. Ingest public OpenAPI specs (APIs.guru, Postman public workspaces) as unclaimed, mock-only listings served through the keyless `/mock` carve-out. The owner then claims the listing, sets `x-zevium-cost`, passes KYC, and earns 95%. This copies Postman's import-what-you-have leverage ([Postman](https://learning.postman.com/latest-v-12/docs/postman-ai/mcp-servers/generate)) and Bazaar's index-on-first-payment seeding ([CDP](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/seller/get-discovered)). Nobody has verified that scraping specs this way is legal under each provider's ToS, so check before proxying anything real. **Negotiated house listings** come next: OpenRouter resells Exa and Parallel under platform deals, so seed the top suppliers the same way before self-serve volume arrives. Next, a **founding-publisher deal with a written fee lock**. MCP-Hive and GET4AGENT run 0% founding fees ([get4agent](https://get4agent.com/partners)), and RapidAPI's fee hike after its acquisition is the trust failure to exploit. Against RapidAPI, 95% beats 75% by **27% more per dollar**. Copy the rest: an `mppx validate`-style conformance CLI ([Stripe MPP](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine/mpp)), a GitHub Action that pushes spec versions, and bounties for tools agents searched for and didn't find, paid per qualifying publisher with real paid calls rather than per listing.

### Demand: be the wallet agents already hold

**The Claude Connectors Directory is the highest-leverage free channel.** Every listing is automatically eligible for Suggested Connectors, which are in-chat recommendations ranked by usage "similar to other app stores" ([claude.com](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/directory)). Self-submission reportedly opened around 2026-09-25 ([BornCity](https://borncity.com/news/anthropic-oeffnet-claude-verzeichnis-entwickler-reichen-plugins-direkt-ein/)). Annotate every tool as readOnly or destructive and publish a privacy policy, the common rejection causes ([Tallyfy](https://mta-sts.tallyfy.com/how-to-list-mcp-server-anthropic-claude-connectors/)). Publish to the official MCP Registry so Glama, Kong, and other aggregators pick Zevium up downstream ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2025-09-08-mcp-registry-preview)). Go further and expose a Zevium sub-registry that speaks the open registry API. Register with Stripe's Directory, which asks for an `llms.txt` and example prompts ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine)), and test whether Bazaar indexes the gateway. Turn every "insufficient balance" into a sale. Tavily's production 402 already returns `next_actions` with an `agentic_payment` path to "continue immediately" ([Tavily](https://docs.tavily.com/documentation/machine-payments/x402)). Copy OpenRouter's **app attribution loop**: optional app headers feed public daily and weekly leaderboards plus a per-API "top apps" tab ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/docs/app-attribution)). Once volume exists, publish a metadata-only "State of Agent APIs" report, the way OpenRouter's 100T-token study with a16z became earned media ([a16z](https://www.a16z.com/state-of-ai)).

### Trust: sell "your agent cannot overspend" and measured quality

x402 has "no spending limit primitive at the protocol layer" ([WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/x402-vs-stripe-mpp-how-to-choose-payment-infrastructure-for-ai-agents-and-mcp-tools-in-2026)). RapidAPI is criticized for having no spend limits ([Apify blog](https://blog.apify.com/best-rapidapi-alternatives/)). Snowflake makes a monthly maximum mandatory on dynamic plans ([Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/collaboration/provider-listings-pricing-model)). Build the wallet DO into **per-key allowances, API allowlists, and max-per-call caps**, the same idea as Cloudflare's virtual wallets ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/cloudflare-x402-agent-spending/)). Then ship the Cloudflare-style **"up to $X" variable price**: the DO pre-authorizes the cap and settles actual cost. **Don't charge for failure.** Zapier bills 0 tasks for failed calls ([Latenode](https://latenode.com/blog/zapier-mcp-pricing)), Firecrawl doesn't bill failed scrapes ([Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing)), and Anthropic doesn't bill failed searches ([Anthropic](https://docs.anthropic.com/it/docs/about-claude/pricing)). Refund credits automatically on upstream 5xx and timeouts.

**Quality measured at the gateway** is the neutral-grading claim that Arcade-plus-Smithery no longer has and that Glama's star- and download-based grades can't match ([Glama](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers)). Publish success rate, p95 latency, uptime, and cost per successful call for each operation, and rank `search_apis` results by them. Require a "use when" line and an output schema on every listing, since Bazaar's 94% gap shows nobody else enforces it. Two agent-ergonomics moves are worth adding. First, a **Code Mode `run_script` tool**: Cloudflare cut 52 tools from about 9,400 tokens to about 600 by collapsing them into search plus execute in Dynamic Workers ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/enterprise-mcp)), and that sandbox is native to Zevium's stack, with each `call_api` inside it still gated by the wallet. Second, an Apify-style `?tools=` pin parameter ([Apify MCP](https://docs.apify.com/platform/integrations/mcp)). Category **auto-routing** across substitutable suppliers using OpenRouter's inverse-square price weighting and 30-second outage skip ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection)) needs normalized category schemas, so it waits until the wedge has several suppliers. Last, on pricing grammar: graduated tiers (Zuplo) and cost-plus pricing for APIs that wrap LLMs (Stripe token billing) are parity gaps if `x-zevium-cost` is only a flat number.

One open decision: OpenRouter and Cloudflare charge their ~5% on top of consumer top-ups and pass unit prices through untouched. Zevium currently takes 5% off the publisher's side. Charging on top-up collects revenue up front and leaves list prices visibly clean. It also changes the "publishers keep 95%" headline, so decide which story wins.

Two of these moves collide with Zevium's own product rules. Keyless wallet sessions only satisfy "every call key-authenticated" if a signed x402 wallet session counts as the key. BYOK passthrough has to stay metered, as OpenRouter's 5% above $25K/month is, or it breaks the "no unmetered execution" rule. Four things remain unknown: whether Anthropic's directory policy allows credit-gated connectors, whether Stripe-settled x402 endpoints show up in Bazaar, whether Exa, Tavily, and Brave ToS permit resale, and how much demand marketplaces actually deliver compared with self-hosting. No source quantified the last one, and Zevium's whole pitch rests on it.

## Conclusion

Discovery is being bundled into payment rails. Coinbase has Bazaar and Agentic.market, Stripe has its Directory and now OpenRouter, Cloudflare is building the Monetization Gateway. Each rail wants to own the catalog too, and each catalog is weak: unvetted, crypto-only, or LLM-only. Zevium shouldn't become another rail. It should be the curated, measured, spec-priced catalog that takes payment over every rail and settles to publishers in fiat. One bold move (inference, not sourced): publish `x-zevium-cost` and `x-zevium-free-tier` as an open OpenAPI pricing extension that Zuplo, Cloudflare rules, and Bazaar indexers could read. That makes Zevium the reference implementation of a standard rather than a feature someone else can copy.

At zero users, the product to sell first is the buyer-side promise: one wallet, a hard cap, and quality data you can trust. Apify and OpenRouter both show publishers arrive after the wallets already hold money. Building on Stripe Connect and MPP keeps Zevium a complement to the most likely threat, and the OpenRouter deal shows Stripe pays billions for the aggregator that owns the demand side.
